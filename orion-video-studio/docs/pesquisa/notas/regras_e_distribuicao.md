# YouTube rules and distribution for a "made for kids" AI channel (Brazil, literacy songs and curiosities, 5–12 min, scheduled publishing), as of Nov 2026

> Method note: in this research session, network access to support.google.com, developers.google.com, planalto.gov.br and tubefilter.com was blocked (DNS failure), so I could not fetch official pages directly. The findings below come from search-result extracts of those pages plus secondary coverage. Items marked **[verify]** should be checked against the primary source before publication. Most results came from secondary sources (blogs, law-firm summaries, news), so confidence is moderate.

## 1. Made for Kids (MFK) designation: obligations, disabled features, discovery, monetization, COPPA/FTC

### Takeaway
A channel of literacy songs for children must be set as "made for kids". That setting removes comments, the notification bell, end screens, cards, memberships and personalized ads, and in exchange the content can reach the YouTube Kids app and kids-oriented recommendations. Ads still run but are contextual, so RPM is lower. Wrong designation exposes the creator, not only YouTube, to FTC/COPPA penalties of up to US$42,530 per violation (US law; a Brazilian creator's real exposure is unclear).

### Cited Findings
- The features restricted on MFK content are consistently listed as comments, end screens, cards, the notification bell, channel memberships and "playlist inclusion"/save to playlist, with personalized ads replaced by contextual ads. A vidIQ guide updated April 2026 repeats the list and adds that MFK videos can still appear in the YouTube Kids app — [vidIQ, "Made for Kids YouTube: How to Make Money in 2026"](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/make-money-kids-youtube-channel/); [Cartoon Brew on COPPA](https://cartoonbrew.com/artist-rights/a-new-youtube-rule-is-threatening-animation-content-creators-heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-coppa-182883.html); [BroadcastBeat](https://www.broadcastbeat.com/how-coppa-affects-online-video)
  - **Conflict/nuance:** some sources say "playlist inclusion" is disabled. My understanding is that viewers cannot *save* MFK videos to their own playlists, while creators can still build playlists of their own MFK videos. **[verify]** on Help Center article 9528076 ("Determining if your content is made for kids").
- The FTC can seek civil penalties "up to $42,530 per violation". The figure is a ceiling, and the FTC weighs factors such as the channel's financial condition — [vidIQ video summary on FTC FAQ](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/youtube-coppa-ftc-42-530-how-likely?t=425); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/83422-youtube-rolls-out-new-controls-aimed-controlling-children.html). (The figure is inflation-adjusted every year, so the 2026 amount may be slightly higher. **[verify]**)
- One lawyer interviewed by vidIQ thought the FTC was unlikely to rush into fining individual creators. This is opinion only — [vidIQ legal insights](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/ftc-coppa-and-youtube-the-legal-insights).
- The FTC's US$10M COPPA settlement with Disney (2025) over videos uploaded without the correct MFK labeling was read as putting content creators "on notice" — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1708356/ftc-puts-content-creators-on-notice-in-%2410-million-coppa-settlement-with-disney).
- Content that follows YouTube's high-quality kids principles "will be recommended more by the YouTube algorithm, in addition to being included in YouTube Kids" — [YouTube Help, Best practices for kids & family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en) (via search extract).

### Inferences
- Without the bell or end screens, discovery depends on (a) browse and suggested recommendations on main YouTube and the YouTube Kids app, (b) autoplay and suggested next videos, (c) search by parents, and (d) playlists and long compilations that keep autoplay sessions going. Packaging for *parents* (search titles) and for *kids* (thumbnail recognition) matters more than subscriber notifications.
- The channel should set MFK at the channel level (and `selfDeclaredMadeForKids=true` per upload via the API) to avoid mixed signals. YouTube's classifier can override the setting either way.
- Lower RPM because ads are contextual only is widely reported, but I found no reliable 2026 number for Brazil.

### Gaps
- I could not read the official Help Center list of disabled features (article 9528076) directly. Mini-player, autoplay-on-home, live chat and stories restrictions were not confirmed.
- No 2026 figures for MFK RPM/CPM in Brazil.
- How COPPA applies to a non-US creator: reachable in principle because the audience includes US children, but I found no sources on practical enforcement against foreign channels.

## 2. YouTube Kids eligibility, quality principles, YPP (inauthentic content 2025/2026), AI disclosure, 2026 AI-kids pressure, AI music/Content ID

### Takeaway
Quality principles set in 2021 decide both YouTube Kids inclusion and YPP standing for kids channels. Low-quality, commercial, "negative behavior" or template-like content can get a whole channel suspended from YPP. The July 2025 rename ("repetitive" to "inauthentic content") and the July 2026 clarification ("Generic or Repetitive", "Unsatisfying or Off-putting", "AI personas on sensitive topics") judge channels on template-like mass production, not on AI use as such. Under pressure from the Fairplay letter of April 2026, YouTube said it limits AI content in YouTube Kids "to a small set of high-quality channels". A new AI-heavy kids channel should therefore expect YouTube Kids inclusion to be hard.

### Cited Findings
**Quality principles / YouTube Kids**
- YouTube developed its kids quality principles with child development specialists. They drive inclusion in YouTube Kids and recommendations on main YouTube. Channels with mostly low-quality kids content (examples: "heavily commercial or promotional", "encouraging negative behaviors or attitudes") can be suspended from YPP. Individual violating videos may get limited or no ads. The list is non-exhaustive — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en); [YouTube Blog, responsibility approach for kids](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/our-responsibility-approach-protecting-kids-and-families-youtube/); [TechCrunch, Oct 25 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/25/youtube-warns-creators-it-will-start-to-demonetize-low-quality-kids-videos-next-month).
- "Heavily commercial" includes content that mainly features products, brands or logos (toys, food) or encourages excessive buying (unboxings) — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en).
- Every channel applying to YPP is reviewed by a trained rater — same source.
- Critics call "low/high quality" deliberately vague and subjective — [Input Mag](https://www.inputmag.com/culture/youtube-warns-creators-of-kids-content-to-shape-up-or-face-demonetization).
- High-quality principles, from my background knowledge of the Help page and **not re-verified this session [verify]**: age-appropriate, enriching, engaging, inspiring; promoting being a good person; learning and inspiring curiosity; encouraging creativity, play and imagination; interaction with the real world. Low-quality examples also include "deceptive or sensational/clickbait" titles and thumbnails, "strange use of children's characters" and "heavily commercial" content.

**YPP inauthentic content (July 15, 2025)**
- Effective July 15, 2025, "repetitive content" was renamed "inauthentic content". YouTube framed this as a clarification: mass-produced or repetitious content was never monetizable. Examples are videos with only slight variations and content mass-produced from the same template. It is enforced at the channel level. Reused content (compilations, clips) is a separate policy and is monetizable with significant original added value — [TechCrunch, Jul 9 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-clarifies-monetisation-policy-targeting-inauthentic-content-9493155).

**July 2026 clarification**
- Tubefilter (July 13, 2026) reports that YouTube renamed the section "Generic or Repetitive Content", saying "this is not a new policy, and our rules around content monetization have not changed". The guidance now has three non-monetizable categories: (1) **Generic or repetitive**, content that "looks like it's made with a template, or that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel"; (2) **Unsatisfying or off-putting**, e.g. channels relying on generic templates or emotionally manipulative themes such as repeated animals in exaggerated distress; (3) **AI personas related to sensitive topics** (synthetic "experts" on health, finance or legal) — [Tubefilter AMP, Jul 13 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/amp/) (search extract only); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-ai-content-rules-monetization/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube-ads/youtube-studio-insights-inauthentic-content/).
- **Date conflict:** Tubefilter dates it July 13, 2026. Secondary blogs cite TechCrunch for July 16, 2026 — [CreatorBlade](https://creatorblade.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026-stay-monetized); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026). I did not find the TechCrunch article itself.
- **Framing conflict:** Tubefilter calls "unsatisfying/off-putting" a new category of already non-monetizable content. YouTube and Metricool call the whole update a clarification, not a new rule.
- The focus is on how content is made and whether it adds value, not on AI use itself — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/youtube-ads/youtube-studio-insights-inauthentic-content/).
- A single blog reports an Oct 1, 2026 Shorts ranking change that reduces reach for reused content. Unverified — [OutlierKit AI crackdown](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-ai-crackdown).

**AI disclosure (altered or synthetic content)**
- Disclosure is required when content "could reasonably be believed to be real by a viewer" (e.g. altered footage of real events, realistic synthetic events). It is not required for clearly unrealistic content such as **animation**, or for colour adjustments, special effects or beauty filters. YouTube says disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en-GB) (search extract); [AlternativeTo, Mar 2024](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/3/youtube-now-requires-creators-to-disclose-when-ai-generated-content-is-used-in-videos/).
- YouTube may apply the label itself (content from its own AI tools, C2PA credentials, detection) — [singhamandeep.com](https://singhamandeep.com/containssyntheticmedia/) (blog; **[verify]**).
- **Synthetic voices:** a realistic AI voice of a real person needs disclosure. A cartoon narrator voice in an animated video is arguably "clearly unrealistic" (inference; no source addressed kids voices specifically).

**2026 pressure on AI kids content**
- April 1, 2026: Fairplay sent an open letter (more than 200 experts, including Jonathan Haidt; more than 135 organizations) to Neal Mohan and Sundar Pichai. Its demands: label all AI content; ban AI-generated content from YouTube Kids; prohibit AI-generated MFK content on main YouTube; stop recommending AI content to under-18s; add a parental AI-off toggle, off by default; halt investment in AI kids content. It cites a report that 40% of recommended videos following popular preschool shows (e.g. Cocomelon) contained AI content — [Fairplay press release](https://fairplayforkids.org/youtube-stop-ai-slop-for-kids-says-letter-from-fairplay-over-200-experts-including-jonathan-haidt/); [letter PDF](https://fairplayforkids.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/YouTube-Letter-AI-Slop.pdf); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/01/youtube-fairplay-kids-ai-open-letter/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91519733/youtube-urged-hundreds-experts-protect-kids-ai-slop-videos).
- YouTube's response: "high standards for content in YouTube Kids, including limiting AI-generated content in the app to a small set of high-quality channels". It also said it labels content from its own AI tools, requires disclosure of realistic AI, and is "actively working on developing AI labels for YouTube Kids" (per AP) — [US News/AP, Apr 1 2026](https://www.usnews.com/news/us/articles/2026-04-01/advocacy-groups-urge-youtube-to-protect-kids-from-ai-slop-videos); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414000/google-pressured-to-stop-serving-ai-video-to-kids.html).
- Context: the letter followed a California jury verdict in a social-media addiction trial finding that YouTube designed its platform to hook young users — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google).
- I found no reporting that YouTube has adopted the letter's demands (as of the search).

**AI music / Content ID**
- Content ID matches audio fingerprints. It does not detect AI generation. Original Suno tracks can still be claimed if they resemble existing works, or if someone else registers the same or similar output — [sunowatermark.com](https://sunowatermark.com/youtube-content-id-ai-music/); [jackrighteous.com](https://jackrighteous.com/blogs/ai-music-distribution-guide/stolen-ai-music-content-id-claim-recovery) (blogs; anecdotal).
- Anecdotal claim: Content ID claimants often require publishing/PRO registration that AI tracks lack; a distributor's Content ID opt-in does not prove YouTube exclusivity eligibility — [reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1skmarx/snapshots/2026-04-18T02%3A00%3A04.501591Z); [OutlierKit AI music 2026](https://alpha.outlierkit.com/resources/ai-generated-music-youtube-monetization-2026/).
- The Munich court ruled largely for GEMA against Suno on July 31, 2026 (headline only) — [CMS case tracker](https://cms.law/en/are/publication/artificial-intelligence-and-copyright-case-tracker/germany-gema-v.-suno-31-july-2026).

### Inferences
- The biggest monetization risk for an automated AI literacy-song channel is the channel-level "generic/repetitive, template-like" judgment (e.g. identical song structure with only the letter swapped), not the AI label. Mitigations: varied formats and arrangements, recurring original characters with continuity, real pedagogical design (phonics progression, call-and-response), and visible human editorial work.
- YouTube Kids inclusion for a new AI-heavy channel is uncertain given the "small set of high-quality channels" statement. Plan growth on main YouTube's MFK recommendations and treat YouTube Kids as a bonus.
- Self-generated AI music: register nothing in Content ID that could misfire, keep generation logs and licenses, and avoid melodies close to traditional or public-domain songs that others may have claimed (e.g. "ABC song" melody). Public-domain melodies themselves are fine, but others' *recordings* of them are in Content ID.
- Animated, clearly cartoon AI content likely needs no "altered or synthetic" label under current rules. Policy may change for YouTube Kids (AI labels in development).

### Gaps
- I could not verify the official wording of the July 2026 Help page or whether it mentions kids content specifically.
- No public criteria for YouTube Kids inclusion of AI channels ("small set of high-quality channels" is undefined).
- No official YouTube statement on AI-generated songs or voices in kids content.

## 3. Packaging and distribution in the kids niche (titles, thumbnails, compilations, playlists, multi-language, Shorts, frequency, timing)

### Takeaway
Leading channels (Cocomelon and similar) rely on organic search/SEO-style titles, a small pool of original songs recycled into long compilations (30+ minutes, opening with the most popular song), and spin-off language channels or multi-language audio. Reliable data on kids' viewing hours and kids-specific thumbnail rules was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Cocomelon compilations are "typically longer than 30 minutes", play songs in succession to entertain kids longer, and begin with a popular song — [Cocomelon case study PDF, Q1 2021](https://global-uploads.webflow.com/5e459e61116466aa9af84140/60148cb00d23a0678c33798e__How%20CoComelon%20Became%20The%20Third%20Largest%20Channel%20on%20YouTube%20%E2%80%94%C2%A0Cocomelon%20%E2%80%94%C2%A0Kids%20Content%20%E2%80%94%20Q1%202021.pdf).
- Most of Cocomelon's video hours come from compilations or reuse of a small pool of original material. It spends minimal ad money and relies on organic traffic and SEO — [RPubs student analysis](https://rstudio-pubs-static.s3.amazonaws.com/1333544_e645aacb5a7045699bab249a86ac4d10.html) (student work; moderate reliability).
- Cocomelon runs language channels ("CoComelon en Español"; Mandarin, Arabic and German added at one point) and the spin-off Nina's Familia (2023) — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/inclusive-youtube-strategy?BBPage=1); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/what-you-can-learn-from-the-3-most-subscribed-youtube-channels-to-get-more-subscribers/).
- YouTube's multi-language audio tracks: manual multi-audio tests began in 2023, with wider access and auto-dubbing integration by mid-2025 — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/how-youtubes-multi-audio-feature-changes-global-growth-strategy). YouTube said testers saw 15% of watch time from non-primary-language views. This figure is secondhand — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/inclusive-youtube-strategy?BBPage=1). One vendor's "20–35%" figure is unsourced; don't use it.
- General packaging (YouTube's own guidance): viewers see the thumbnail and title first; YouTube's Test & Compare tests up to 3 thumbnails per video; thumbnails should be 1280×720, 16:9, simple and readable on mobile; title and thumbnail should complement, not repeat, each other — [YouTube Help, Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en-GB); [vidIQ thumbnail tips 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-tips/).
- About 50–60 character titles with one keyword and a hook — [CMU web best practices](https://www.cmu.edu/web/best-practices/youtube-content.html).
- The kids quality principles count "deceptive/sensational" titles and thumbnails as low quality **[verify]** — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en).

### Inferences
- Title pattern in pt-BR, adapting the global "ABC Song | Phonics Song for Kids" pattern: `[Topic] | Música do Alfabeto para Crianças | [Brand]`, or `Aprenda as Vogais A E I O U | Música Infantil Educativa`. The parent-search keyword goes first and the brand last. For compilations: `Músicas do Alfabeto — 45 min | Compilação Infantil`.
- Thumbnails: one recognizable recurring character plus one huge letter or object, saturated colours, little or no text (pre-readers), consistent style across the series. Avoid shock faces or "strange character use" (a low-quality flag).
- Compilations: 30 min to 1 h (and some 2–3 h) are standard. Under the reused-content rule they are monetizable when they are the channel's own original songs, but making them the bulk of uploads increases "repetitive" risk. Keep new originals flowing and compile periodically.
- Playlists by learning goal (Vogais, Alfabeto, Números, Sílabas, Curiosidades) support autoplay sessions and SEO.
- Multi-language: start in pt-BR, then add Spanish and English audio tracks to the same video (multi-language audio) or separate channels. Separate channels follow the Cocomelon model; audio tracks keep views consolidated.
- Shorts: likely discovery (song hooks of 15–60 s), but MFK Shorts get fewer engagement features. No reliable kids-specific data found.
- Schedule: consistency (e.g. 2–3 long videos per week plus a monthly compilation) is common advice, but no source here. Kids' viewing-time peaks (mornings, after school, weekends) are intuitive but **unsourced**.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on when Brazilian children watch YouTube (hours/days), or on optimal upload frequency for kids channels.
- No authoritative kids-specific title/thumbnail studies. Results for nursery rhyme queries were spam domains and were discarded.
- No data on Shorts' effectiveness for MFK channels in 2025–2026.

## 4. YouTube Data API for automated scheduled uploads

### Takeaway
Uploads from an **unaudited** API project created after July 28, 2020 are locked to private, so a compliance audit is mandatory before any automated publishing goes public. The `videos.insert` quota cost changed recently: it fell from about 1,600 units to about 100 (Dec 2025 per secondary sources), and some sources describe a further change to a separate upload bucket (around 100 uploads/day, about 1 unit) in mid-2026. **This is conflicting; confirm on the official revision history.** The relevant fields are `status.selfDeclaredMadeForKids`, `status.containsSyntheticMedia` (added Oct 30, 2024 per a blog), `status.privacyStatus=private` plus `status.publishAt` for scheduling, `thumbnails.set` and `playlistItems.insert`.

### Cited Findings
- "All videos uploaded via the videos.insert endpoint from unverified API projects created after 28 July 2020 will be restricted to private viewing mode. To lift this restriction, each project must undergo an audit to verify compliance with the Terms of Service" (quoted at the top of the videos.insert reference). Setting privacyStatus doesn't override it. Locked videos reportedly can't be appealed and must be re-uploaded via an audited project ("permanent" is a single-blog claim **[verify]**) — [RapidDev](https://rapidevelopers.com/api-automations/how-to-automate-youtube-video-uploads-using-the-api); [singhamandeep.com, YouTube Data API 2026](https://singhamandeep.com/?p=1570).
- **Quota, conflicting:**
  - Legacy figure: about 1,600 units per videos.insert (10,000/day default means about 6 uploads/day). Still cited by many guides; outdated — [Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/is-the-youtube-api-free-in-2026-quota-limits-costs-when-to-pay).
  - Revision history entry dated Dec 4, 2025 reportedly reduces videos.insert from about 1,600 to about 100 units — [Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/blog/youtube-api-pricing); [ChannelCrawler, Mar 10 2026](https://channelcrawler.com/insights/youtube-api-daily-limit-quotas-costs-and-how-to-scale-beyond-10000-units-channelcrawler).
  - Newer claim: documentation now presents videos.insert as a separate default bucket of 100 calls/day, and one source says the cost fell to 1 unit on June 1, 2026 (table dated Oct 22, 2026) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-api-quota/); [bundle.social, Aug 4 2026](https://bundle.social/blog/youtube-api-quota-exceeded-limits-fixes). **Not verified against Google.**
- `status.containsSyntheticMedia` added Oct 30, 2024, settable on videos.insert and videos.update — [singhamandeep.com](https://singhamandeep.com/containssyntheticmedia/) (blog **[verify]**).
- Quota and the private lock are separate systems — [singhamandeep.com](https://singhamandeep.com/?p=1570).

### Inferences
- Pipeline: `videos.insert` (part=snippet,status; `status.privacyStatus="private"`, `status.publishAt=<ISO 8601 UTC>`, `status.selfDeclaredMadeForKids=true`, `status.containsSyntheticMedia` set only when realistic content requires it, `snippet.defaultLanguage/defaultAudioLanguage="pt-BR"`, `snippet.categoryId` e.g. 27 Education or 10 Music), then `thumbnails.set` (requires a verified channel for custom thumbnails; about 50 units historically **[verify]**), then `playlistItems.insert` (about 50 units historically). From background knowledge: `publishAt` only works while the video is private and has never been published **[verify]**.
- Multi-language audio tracks and Test & Compare thumbnails are, to my knowledge, Studio-only and not exposed in the Data API **[verify]**.
- Before the audit passes, test with private uploads only. Budget weeks for the audit.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch developers.google.com (blocked), so the current videos.insert quota (100 vs 1 unit vs separate bucket) and the exact publishAt/thumbnails.set costs remain unverified.
- Audit turnaround times in 2026: no source found.

## 5. Brazil: ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025), CONANDA 163/2014, LGPD

### Takeaway
The ECA Digital was signed Sept 17, 2025 and has been in force since **March 17, 2026** (6-month adaptation via MP 1.319/2025). It targets tech providers (platforms, apps, games) more than creators, but it bans profiling minors for targeted ads, bans loot boxes, and bans monetizing or boosting content that portrays children in a sexualized/adultized way. Law firms say every agent in the digital chain can be held liable. CONANDA Res. 163/2014 treats advertising or marketing communication directed at children as abusive, which matters for any brand integration, merch call-to-action or "peça para seus pais" in videos.

### Cited Findings
- Signed Sept 17, 2025. The president vetoed the 1-year vacatio legis and set 6 months via MP 1.319/2025, so the law has been in force since March 17, 2026 — [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/estatuto-digital-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente-lei-n-15-211-2025-entra-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026); [Poder360](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-governo/eca-digital-entra-em-vigor-nesta-3a-feira-entenda-o-que-muda/).
- The ANPD is the enforcer and plans gradual implementation with monitoring in 2026. A regulatory decree was reportedly signed March 18, 2026 — [Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/eca-digital-sancionada-lei/); [Mayer Brown, Apr 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies).
- Sanctions range from a warning (up to 30 days to correct) to a fine of up to 10% of Brazilian group revenue, capped at R$50 million per infraction — [Machado Meyer factsheet](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/images/ebooks/Factsheet_ECA_Digital.pdf).
- Ban on profiling minors for commercial advertising, including emotional analysis and AR/VR/XR for that purpose (cited as art. 22) — [Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/protecao-de-criancas-e-adolescentes-no-ambiente-digital/); [Meio & Mensagem/ProXXIma](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/proxxima/o-novo-estatuto-digital-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente-e-seus-efeitos). **Article numbering varies between sources [verify]**.
- Ban on loot boxes in games directed at or likely accessed by minors (art. 20). The MJSP asked the ANPD to monitor non-compliant games — [Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/protecao-de-criancas-e-adolescentes-no-ambiente-digital/); [gov.br MJSP technical note](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/noticias-1/mjsp-pede-a-anpd-que-monitore-jogos-eletronicos-com-caixas-de-recompensa-que-nao-implementaram-verificacao-de-idade/NotaTcnican55.pdf).
- "Veda a monetização ou impulsionamento de conteúdos que retratem crianças e adolescentes de forma erotizada" (cited as art. 26). This directly affects creators who exploit children in adult-appeal situations — [Demarest](https://www.demarest.com.br/protecao-de-criancas-e-adolescentes-no-ambiente-digital/); [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/colunas/eca-digital-o-que-realmente-muda-na-sua-vida/).
- Obligations are not limited to direct providers. All agents in the digital chain may be liable — [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/estatuto-digital-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente-lei-n-15-211-2025-entra-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026).
- A provision criticized as burdensome to creators and the entertainment sector was removed during passage (not identified) — [Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2025-out-21/eca-digital-e-marco-da-protecao-online-de-criancas-e-adolescentes//?print=1).
- CONANDA Res. 163 (approved March 13, 2014; published DOU April 4, 2014) deems abusive any advertising or marketing communication directed at children intended to persuade them to consume. Listed techniques include children's language, cartoon characters, children's songs, child presenters, and promotions with games or competitions with child appeal (art. 2). It is grounded in the ECA and CDC; its constitutionality and binding force are disputed by the advertising sector — [Res. 163 PDF (MPBA)](https://mpba.mp.br/sites/default/files/biblioteca/crianca-e-adolescente/publicidade-e-consumo-na-infancia/conanda/resolucao_163_conanda.pdf); [Jus.com.br](https://jus.com.br/artigos/58839/a-resolucao-n-163-do-conselho-nacional-dos-direitos-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente); [Migalhas](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/203254/sim-a-publicidade-voltada-as-criancas-e-abusiva-e-ilegal).
- Res. 163, the CDC, the ECA and Conar guidelines apply to child influencers promoting brands — [Galicia Educação](https://www.galiciaeducacao.com.br/blog/protecao-juridica-das-criancas-frente-a-publicidade-digital-e-adultizacao/).

### Inferences
- For a pure-animation AI literacy channel without child actors, the ECA Digital's direct creator-facing risks are low. The main exposures are (a) any sponsored or brand content or merch pitch aimed at kids (Res. 163 plus CDC art. 37 §2 abusive advertising), (b) collecting children's data off-platform (e.g. a site, app, WhatsApp list or contest), which is covered by LGPD art. 14 (best interest, specific parental consent for under-12s) and ECA Digital obligations if the creator operates its own app or site, and (c) never depicting children in adultized ways.
- Ad targeting on YouTube is the platform's responsibility. MFK's contextual-only ads already align with the profiling ban.
- If the channel later builds an app, game or site for kids, it becomes a "fornecedor" directly subject to the ECA Digital (age verification, parental supervision tools, privacy by default, ANPD oversight).
